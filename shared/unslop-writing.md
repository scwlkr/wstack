# Unslop writing

Edit existing prose so it is specific, useful and natural for its audience.
Patterns are review prompts, never evidence of AI authorship or automatic bans.
Leave effective writing alone, even when it uses a listed word or structure.

## Editing pass

1. Read the requested text and enough context to identify its purpose, audience
   and intended voice. Follow supplied editorial guidance.
2. Find concrete problems below. Change only passages where a revision improves
   meaning, accuracy, clarity or rhythm; preserve the author's useful personality.
3. Read the revision in context. Check facts, qualifications, terminology and
   tone against the original. Stop when another pass would only change taste.
4. Deliver the edited artifact, with a brief explanation of consequential edits
   when useful. If the request is review-only, report findings without rewriting.

## Patterns and examples

Examples assume their facts are supplied. Never invent details to make a rewrite
more specific.

- **Inflated significance.** Praise such as "pivotal" or "groundbreaking" needs
  substance. "A pivotal improvement to exports" -> "Exports now include archived
  orders." Keep a claim of importance when the text explains its consequences.
- **Vague benefits.** Replace promises with the actual capability. "Seamlessly
  streamline your workflow" -> "Assign leads and see who needs a follow-up."
  Preserve expressive language when it carries the intended voice or meaning.
- **Empty introductions.** Start with information the reader needs. Delete
  "In today's fast-paced world" when it contributes no relevant context.
- **Unsupported authority.** "Experts agree this is the best approach" needs
  an identified source and supported claim. Cite the supplied evidence, qualify
  the claim or remove it; do not invent a source or erase necessary uncertainty.
- **Contrast formulas.** "It's not just a calendar; it's a productivity partner"
  -> "See deadlines and available time in one calendar." Keep contrasts that
  clarify a real distinction, such as local versus shared calendars.
- **Forced lists and repeated cadence.** Do not add a third benefit to complete
  a trio or give every paragraph the same short-long-short rhythm. Two supported
  benefits can remain two; a useful three-step procedure can remain three.
- **Jargon and noun piles.** "Leverage robust capabilities for the implementation
  of validation" -> "Use these checks to validate the import." Keep precise
  domain terms when the audience needs them; explain unfamiliar ones briefly.
- **Synonym cycling.** Calling the same thing a "workspace", "hub" and "portal"
  makes readers infer differences. Use "workspace" consistently unless the terms
  name distinct things.
- **Stacked hedges.** "This could potentially reduce delays" -> "This may reduce
  delays." Preserve confidence levels, conditions and caveats that affect the
  reader's decision; do not turn "may" into "will" for a stronger voice.
- **Formatting as a template.** If every sentence has a bold label, emoji or
  dramatic aside, simplify the parts that interrupt reading. "**Export:** Export
  your orders" -> "Export your orders." Keep useful headings and scan aids.
- **Mechanical punctuation and transitions.** Repeated em dashes, colons or
  "Additionally" openings can flatten rhythm. "Additionally, backups run nightly"
  -> "Backups run nightly." Punctuation, curly quotes and common words stay valid
  when they fit the publication and sentence.
- **Chatbot praise and generic endings.** Delete "Great question!" when it delays
  the answer. Replace "The future looks bright" with a supported next step, or
  simply end. Keep summaries that help readers act or recall a long argument.

## Preserve

- Facts, citations, quotations, necessary detail and the intended meaning.
  Do not silently rewrite quoted language or weaken useful caveats.
- The author's voice and the artifact's purpose. Marketing can be persuasive;
  technical instructions can be structured; neither needs forced informality.
- Honest authorship. Do not add personal experience, opinions, slang, mistakes
  or randomness to simulate a human author. Surface missing facts when needed.

Do not turn the checklist into a banned-word search or rewrite good passages
just to produce visible changes.
